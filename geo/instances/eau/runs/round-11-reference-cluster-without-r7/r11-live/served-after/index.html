01 / The idea

What if the training
system could
learn, too?

An agent’s learning signal is only as reliable as the interaction that produces it.

Real conversations are rarely fully specified. Agents need to ask questions, uncover intent, and work with tools across multiple turns. But simulated users can be inconsistent, tools can be unreliable, and rewards can miss meaningful progress.

Most training methods treat that system as fixed. eau makes it editable. A meta-agent reads trajectories, tool traces, and training statistics, then revises the components that shape the next interaction.

Validated updates take effect within an ongoing run. What the system learns about training carries forward into the next one.

02 / The framework

A feedback loop.
With room to change.

The policy learns through reinforcement learning.
The meta-agent adapts the experience it learns from.

More controllable conversations.

Combine explicit symbolic rules with natural LLM responses. The meta-agent can revise disclosure rules, persona instructions, generation settings, and the mixture between the two simulators.

04 / A closer look

Experience becomes
part of the system.

01 / Learning from interventions

Every run leaves something useful.

After diagnosing severe issues and restarting early runs, eau explores interventions within a run and carries the findings forward. The resulting frontier improves beyond the generic meta-agent in this experiment.

02 / Adapting at two timescales

Timing is part of the method.

Starting from the same first four runs of experience, adding within-run intervention reaches 0.623 on UserGym, compared with 0.577 for between-run adaptation alone.

These curves show progression by run count, rather than a comparison under matched total compute. Evaluation conditions remain fixed while the training system evolves.
